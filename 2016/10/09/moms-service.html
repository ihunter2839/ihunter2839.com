Mom's Service

Oct 9, 2016 • Ian Hunter

During the summer, my mother, father and I used to go for long road trips across the country. On one such trip, we found ourselves at Lake Louise in the Alberta province of Canada. We walked along the shore of the lake, in awe of the calm sapphire water and the towering alps in the background. As the sun began to dip in the sky, we made our way back to the car and headed for the nearest public campground. We arrived to a small spot tucked away amidst old growth trees, cut out from the thick green brush that covered the ground. Just like every other night, we routinely set up our tent, rolled out our 32 degree Californian sleeping bags, and made a fire. My favorite part of these road trips was their unpredictability - the spontaneity of the events, places, and scenes. It afforded us a much needed break from the school week cycle, where everything was set into a daily routine. That night at Lake Louise, the unpredictable was the freezing cold. Huddled in our sleeping bags, we put on every piece of clothing that we had brought with us. No patch of skin could be left exposed, and eventually we wrapped ourselves up until only our mouths were visible to the frigid air. Even with these extra layers none of us could sleep, so we shivered our way through the long night. When the sun finally peeked its head over the mountain top, Dad quickly got up, made a fire, and heated some water. He poured the hot water into water bottles, jumped back into the tent, and gave Mom and me a hot water bottle to heat ourselves with. As we all laid there in our tent, curled up in our sleeping bags, frantically trying to heat our extremities with a bottle of hot water, we all had to laugh. Not just a “this is ridiculous” giggle, or a “why did we do this to ourselves” chuckle, but a deep, long laugh that comes from the shared experience of the unpredictable with those that you love. More than anything else, I will miss sharing these kinds of experiences with my mother and being able to laugh with her about the quirks and idiosyncrasies of life that make living enjoyable. And so, although I am devastated by the fact that I will not hear my mother’s laugh again, I am grateful for every minute that I have spent with her.
